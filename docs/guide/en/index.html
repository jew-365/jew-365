Getting started

The first time the app opens it asks a few short questions: name and phone (optional), gender and marital status, your nusach, which mitzvot to track, and a city or a GPS location for the zmanim. Once notifications are allowed, today's mitzvot are waiting on Home.

Moving to a new phone? On the welcome screen tap “I have a backup file” and restore the file exported on the old phone.

Five tabs sit at the bottom: More, Siddur, Home, Tracking and Settings. More holds the schedule, Chok LeYisrael, Tehillim, the hilulot, the prayer compass, the halacha hotline and this guide, which also opens from Settings → About and contact or by searching “guide” in Settings.

The prayer compass in the More tab is a radar: a sweep line turns and the Kotel lights up as a point at its distance and direction from the phone. Under it the screen says how many degrees to turn and which way, the distance in kilometres and the bearing from north, and when the phone faces the Kotel it turns green with a short vibration. It needs the location permission and asks for it when missing. The direction is measured from where you stand; when your location is not found the screen says so, measures from the city in Settings and lets you try again. When it asks for calibration, move the phone in a figure eight.

Home and marking a mitzvah

Home shows the mitzvot whose time is open now, the most urgent first, each with the time left and a progress bar. Each mitzvah appears in its halachic time window, computed for the location you chose. Below them are Next up, what was missed and what is already done today.

Tap the check button on a card to mark the mitzvah done; its other notifications for that day are cancelled. Undo in the Completed list brings it back. A mitzvah whose time has passed can still be marked until the day ends.

Tapping a card opens the mitzvah's screen, and a long press opens quick actions: Details, or Skip today for a day it does not apply. When the mitzvah has a text, Open text opens it in the siddur. A strip at the top flags what needs attention, such as the check-in after Shabbat or a location that was not allowed.

The app's day turns at dawn (alot hashachar), not at midnight: the night belongs to the evening before, so Maariv said after midnight is filed under that evening's day.

Reminders and the reminder editor

Each mitzvah's screen shows today's time window, the next notification and its reminders in the order they go out, each with when it fires next. Add reminder adds one more, and Reset to default brings back the original reminders.

In When to send you choose when the reminder goes out: before the time starts (up to three hours before), at the start or after it, before the time ends or at a set time, and below it how many minutes. Skip if already done drops it once the mitzvah is marked. A custom mitzvah also offers After the end, if not marked, and Kiddush Levana offers After Shabbat or Yom Tov ends.

A mitzvah that is on always keeps at least one reminder: the last one cannot be deleted, and switching on a mitzvah with none asks you to set one first.

In the notification, Done marks the mitzvah without opening the app, and Open text opens its text when it has one. Tapping the notification opens the text, or the mitzvah's screen when there is none. On Android, exempt the app from battery saving in Settings → Device and notifications so reminders arrive on time.

Reminders are set ahead for the coming days even if the app is not opened: about a month on Android and about a week and a half on iPhone, where from the third day each mitzvah keeps one reminder. On the last planned day a notification at 8 pm says your reminders are about to run out, and every time the app opens it plans again.

My mitzvot and custom mitzvot

Settings → My mitzvot lists every mitzvah by group, each with a switch, and tapping a row opens the mitzvah's screen. Mitzvot added in an update are switched on for you, and the days before that never count as missed. A mitzvah not customary for women is off for a woman and marked Not customary for women, and she can still switch it on. The More group also holds hilulot and Rosh Chodesh.

Seasonal mitzvot appear only in their time: the Omer count, Chanukah candles (the reminder names the night), Selichot in your nusach's season, and Ushpizin on the nights of Sukkot with that night's guest. Kiddush Levana appears once a month, from the first night it is said until the period ends, until it is marked. Some mitzvot depend on the nusach.

Add custom mitzvah at the bottom of the screen creates your own mitzvah: a name, hours (an end before the start runs into the next day), and a schedule of set weekdays, a range from date to date or a run of days. You choose whether to skip it on Shabbat and Yom Tov, and add reminders and rich content: text, a blessing, a link or a text from the siddur. When its range or streak ends it switches off, and its history is kept.

Shabbat and Yom Tov

On Shabbat and Yom Tov the app rests: from candle lighting until ten minutes after nightfall no notifications go out, and a quiet screen with the exit time covers the app. This is by design, and the app comes back by itself.

An hour before candle lighting a notification gives the lighting and exit times and, as switched on, the Omer count, hilulot, Rosh Chodesh and the Ushpizin of the nights inside the Shabbat or Yom Tov.

After Shabbat or Yom Tov the app opens a check-in to mark what was done in that time, and a strip on Home reminds you to mark. Notifications remind you at the exit, two hours later and the next evening. You can mark until dawn after the first weekday; anything unmarked by then counts as missed. Shabbat and Yom Tov count in history and the streak like any other day.

The siddur

In the Siddur tab each group opens and closes with a tap on its title: first the texts whose time is open now (open each time you enter the tab), then the meal blessings, the prayers (Shacharit, Mincha and Maariv), the mitzvot (tefillin, the morning Shema, the morning blessings, candle lighting, havdalah and the Omer), then the other blessings and prayers by group. A text not said today is greyed out, with when it is said, and it still opens at any time, in the text of the next day it is said and without Done. Each text is set for the day: Hallel, Musaf and inserts such as Ya'aleh VeYavo appear on their days.

At the top of the reader: Jump to section, Minyan or Alone in prayers with minyan passages (Alone folds Kaddish, Barchu, Kedusha and the Torah reading), larger and smaller text, and auto-scroll at the speed you choose. A folded passage opens on tap. While you read, the controls fold into one line with Back and the text's name, and come back when you scroll up or tap that line. When the app is in English, the source's translation appears under the Hebrew where it has one.

The star button saves the current place as a bookmark, and Bookmarks shows Where you left off and the text's bookmarks, to jump to or delete. Next time the reader offers to continue where you left off: for a mitzvah's text (the prayers, candle lighting, havdalah, the Omer) on the same day only, for every other text always. Done at the end of the text marks its mitzvah, and in a mitzvah's text the settings button at the top opens the mitzvah's screen.

Settings → Prayer and siddur sets the nusach, the Shema opinion (Gra or Magen Avraham, which decides when the morning Shema window ends), Minyan or Alone, the Siddur wording for a man or a woman, and scrolling. The siddur wording follows the gender in your profile: a woman reads Modeh Ani, the morning blessings and the other women's forms.

Selichot download as an add-on in your nusach: until it is installed, its row in the siddur, its mitzvah screen and the More tab offer the download, and a week before the season Home offers it too. Changing your nusach offers the new nusach's Selichot.

Tehillim and Tikkun HaKlali

The whole book of Tehillim is one text, the same in every nusach, opened from the More tab or the General group of the siddur. Jump to section offers the prayer before, the seven days of the weekly division and the prayer after.

Tikkun HaKlali leads the General group of the siddur. In both, the reader remembers where you left off and offers to continue there every time, and bookmarks can be kept.

Chok LeYisrael

Chok LeYisrael is an add-on downloaded once, about 5 MB, from its mitzvah screen or the More tab, and after that it is available without an internet connection. When the download ends, the app asks whether to turn on reminders and tracking. When there is a new version of the text, the mitzvah screen offers the update, and the add-on can be deleted there too.

The reader shows today's portion of the week's parasha, with a row of days to pick another day and, in a double week, a choice of which parasha to learn. While the mitzvah is on, Done marks the day, and earlier unmarked days of the week appear as folded make-ups. Rashi and Bartenura show or fold by Commentary or Text only at the top of the reader or in Settings, and each one opens on tap. The parasha row picks any other parasha to read, without Done, and Back to this week's parasha returns; a bookmark or the last place in another parasha takes you back there.

The mitzvah is open for the learning day, from dawn to the next dawn, and until candle lighting before Shabbat and Yom Tov, with its default reminder at 09:00. Thursday night counts for Thursday or Friday, as you choose on the first Done or in Settings → Prayer and siddur. The text is from the Torat Emet database under CC BY-NC-SA 2.5; the full credit is in Settings → About and contact.

Tracking, streak and the weekly report

The Tracking tab shows your streak and the days left to the next goal above three tabs. Summary shows how much you marked today, this week and this Hebrew month, a calendar of the Hebrew month where each day is shaded by how much of it was done (tap a day to see its mitzvot), and each mitzvah's consistency and streak. Tapping a mitzvah, in the list or inside a day, opens its screen. A mitzvah switched on today reads New until one of its days is counted.

Trends shows the last 12 weeks, your strongest day of the week (Shabbat and Yom Tov apart, since they are marked in the check-in), how each mitzvah changed from the 30 days before, and the weekly reports. Achievements keeps the badges you earned and your records, even after old history is cleared.

The streak counts days in a row with at least one mitzvah marked. A day with nothing marked and something missed breaks it; a Shabbat or Yom Tov still waiting for its check-in does not.

A weekly report opens from the reports list in Trends: what was marked, the best day, which mitzvah to strengthen and what the next week holds, with buttons to move between weeks. About 45 minutes after Shabbat ends a notification says the report is ready; it can be turned off in Settings → Device and notifications.

The schedule

The schedule opens from the More tab, by day, week or month, with arrows to the previous and next. The day view lists the day's zmanim and mitzvot by the hour, and tapping a mitzvah opens it.

Tapping a day in the week or month view opens that day's mitzvot, with arrows to the previous and next day and a count of what was done. That screen is for reading: marks are made on Home, or in the check-in after Shabbat and Yom Tov.

Rosh Chodesh and hilulot

Rosh Chodesh notices and hilula notices are off by default. Switch them on in Settings → My mitzvot, in the More group, or on their own screens.

Each screen lists its notices: for Rosh Chodesh an early notice, the evening before and on the day; for a hilula an early notice and as it begins. Each can be turned off or timed: at or before sunset, or at a set time, and the early notice also how many days ahead. While the notices are on, at least one stays on. The coming dates are listed below. Tapping a tzaddik in the list opens a page about him, and so does tapping the hilula notice.

A notice that falls on Shabbat or Yom Tov is folded into the notification before it begins. These are notices, not mitzvot, so they do not count in history.

Family purity

Family purity tracking is offered only to married users who answered the gender question, and is switched on in Settings → Profile → Family purity. It follows the count, the bedikot, the tevila night and the onot of separation, with reminders by the halachic times.

The family purity screen records an onset, the hefsek tahara, bedikot and the tevila, and shows today's tasks, the coming onot and the month's calendar. Taharah settings choose the custom, who uses it (a woman, or a husband who sees only the onot and the tevila night), discreet notification wording and a biometric lock.

The data is stored encrypted and is not part of the backup. Delete the records starts a new count, and Reset family purity tracking also deletes the settings and turns tracking off. The app does not rule on halacha: on any doubt it stops the count until the rav's instruction is recorded.

Settings: search, backup and reset

Settings opens with a search: type a word such as “notifications”, “nusach” or “backup” to go straight to the setting. It also finds mitzvot, siddur texts and the sections inside them, and the tzaddikim of the hilulot list with their hilula date, and forgives typos and typing on the other language's keyboard.

Its sections: Profile (name, phone, gender and marital status), Prayer and siddur, Device and notifications (city or GPS, notifications, the weekly report, theme, display size and language), Backup, restore and reset, and About and contact. The city or GPS location also decides whether you count as in Israel or abroad, for example for the second day of Yom Tov.

On Android your settings, mitzvot and history are backed up to your Google account, and on iPhone in the device's iCloud backup. Settings → Backup, restore and reset also exports a backup file and restores it on any phone; a restore replaces what is on the phone. Reset the app deletes everything, including the family purity data, which no backup holds.

Updates

Most updates arrive by themselves: the app checks for one each time it opens or comes back, and downloads it in the background. When it is ready a strip says “A new version of the app is ready”, and tapping it loads it. Even with the app in the background, a notification says when an update is ready, while notifications are on and never on Shabbat or Yom Tov.

The version number is in Settings → About and contact, as version-update number. What changed in each update is under What's new at the top of this guide. Changes that need a new version of the app come through the store.

About and contact

Settings → About and contact holds the version, a contact form (name, phone and message, sent to us through Formspree with the app version and device type), the email 365jew@gmail.com, and links to the support page, the privacy policy, the app's store page and the Chok LeYisrael credit.

For a halachic question, the halacha hotline in the More tab dials a line by the nusach in Settings: Ashkenazi lines, and for Edot HaMizrach a line following Maran, by WhatsApp too.

The app does not rule on halacha. The times and rules it applies are given to a rav for review, and each answer changes that one rule. In any doubt, ask a rav.
