Getting started

The first time the app opens it asks a few short questions: name, phone, gender and marital status (all optional, and the answers stay on the device), your nusach, which mitzvot to track, and the place for the zmanim: with location access allowed, the location updates itself every time the app opens, so the times are always those of where the phone is; without it, you pick any locality in Israel or any city in the world by searching its name, and the times stay with it. Then comes a permissions screen that shows what is already allowed and lets you allow the rest. Once notifications are allowed, today's mitzvot are waiting on Home.

Moving to a new phone? On the welcome screen tap “I have a backup file” and restore the file exported on the old phone.

Five tabs sit at the bottom: More, Siddur, Home, Tracking and Settings. More holds the schedule, Chok LeYisrael, Tehillim, the hilulot, the prayer compass, the halacha hotline and this guide, which also opens from Settings → About and contact or by searching “guide” in Settings.

The prayer compass in the More tab is a radar: a sweep line turns and the Kotel lights up as a point at its distance and direction from the phone. Under it the screen says how many degrees to turn and which way, the distance in kilometres and the bearing from north, and when the phone faces the Kotel it turns green with a short vibration. It needs the location permission and asks for it when missing. The direction is measured from where you stand; when your location is not found the screen says so, measures from the city in Settings and lets you try again. When it asks for calibration, move the phone in a figure eight. It also opens from the “Prayer compass” button the siddur shows before every Amidah of Shacharit, Musaf, Mincha and Maariv. Opened from a prayer, it shows a “Continue to the prayer” button at the bottom once you face the Kotel.

Home and marking a mitzvah

At the top of Home is one circle for the day and the night: the sun moves along its upper half from dawn to nightfall and the moon along its lower half until the next dawn, by the zmanim of the Ohr HaChaim luach at the location you chose. The sky changes colour from dawn to night, and under the circle is a see-through glass card in the colour of the sky behind it, with a thin edge, that follows the hour and the preview, with three tabs: Today (what is special today, what the prayer adds, today's Tehillim, and what the board learned from your marks, marked ✦), This week (the parasha, Shabbat's start and end, Shabbat Mevarchim with the molad and what comes this week) and Mine (what you chose in ⋯). The board opens the card that matters most by itself, and tapping a tab keeps your choice. Under it is the carousel of times: the coming times first, with candle lighting, the Chanukah candles and the end of a fast among them; tomorrow's at night; the past ones last. The time that matters fills and shows how long is left, other times running at the same moment carry a thin bar, and touching one shows its progress. The + at the end of the carousel edits its times: remove a time, add times the circle does not show, such as Mincha Ketana, and star favourites, also with a long press on a time in the carousel. A favourite leads the carousel at its next time, tomorrow's too, and Mine shows how long until the next favourite. The same list is in Settings → Halachic times; by default every time of the circle shows. Drag the sun or the moon all the way around the circle, from the day into the night and from the night into the morning, always within the same day, or tap a time in the carousel, to see the circle at another time; Back to now returns it. The lists below stay live.

Open now shows every mitzvah whose time is open, with a ring of the time left and its start and end. Done marks it done and cancels its other notifications for that day, and Open text opens its text in the siddur. Messages shows what needs attention, such as the check-in after Shabbat or a location that was not allowed, along with your streak, the coming Shabbat (from Thursday night), Rosh Chodesh, and hilulot when their notices are on. When no mitzvah is open, the messages take the place of Open now.

My day lists all of today's mitzvot: what is still open, then what is done and last what was missed, in three views you can choose, Rings, Cards or Timeline, and your choice is kept. A missed mitzvah can still be marked until the day ends, and Undo takes a mark back. Tap a mitzvah to open its screen; a long press opens quick actions: Details or Skip today.

The app's day turns at dawn (alot hashachar), not at midnight: the night belongs to the evening before, so Maariv said after midnight is filed under that evening's day.

Reminders and the reminder editor

Each mitzvah's screen shows today's time window, the next notification and its reminders in the order they go out, each with when it fires next. Add reminder adds one more, and any change to the reminders or the nags opens Reset notification timeline on the timeline, which brings back the original reminders and the smart nag. Every reminder also says how long is left and until when the time lasts: on the iPhone under its title, and on Android in the first line of its content. Each mitzvah has exactly one red notification, the last time, which opens with ❗, and one orange before it that opens with ⚠️ (for a prayer the last time with a minyan, for a night mitzvah “ideally before midnight”, for any other mitzvah 15 minutes before the red one), on every phone; all the rest are plain. On Android phones that show the notification colour, the icon is green, orange on the orange one and red on the red one.

In When to send you choose when the reminder goes out: before the time starts (up to three hours before), at the start or after it, before the time ends or at a set time, and below it how many minutes. Skip if already done drops it once the mitzvah is marked. A custom mitzvah also offers After the end, if not marked, and Kiddush Levana offers After Shabbat or Yom Tov ends.

Each reminder goes out once, as set. On top of that, every mitzvah has a “last time to start”: the end of its time less the time it takes (Shacharit 20 minutes alone and 45 with a minyan, Mincha and Maariv 10 alone and 20 with a minyan, the Shema and tefillin 5, Chok LeYisrael 15, and a custom mitzvah what its form says). A prayer sends two of these, with a minyan and alone, at every level. At every level a notification also goes out when the mitzvah’s critical time begins, and the nagging level adds notifications in it: Gentle one, Medium two and Nagging three. By day the critical time is the last third of the time (for a custom mitzvah at most its last 3 hours, and like Maariv for hours past midnight); for Maariv and the Omer from the start of the time to midnight; for the bedtime Shema the two hours before midnight, after its 21:00 reminder; for Chanukah the first hour of lighting. For the night mitzvot the orange is “ideally before midnight”, and between midnight and dawn only the red goes out, the real last time, half an hour before dawn. The wording follows the mitzvah: “Last time to put on tefillin”, “Ideally, count before midnight” and so on. Two notifications of one mitzvah are at least 10 minutes apart, and nothing goes out after Done, on Shabbat and Yom Tov, or in the quiet hours. Choose the level in Settings → Device and notifications, and any mitzvah can have a level of its own on its screen. On Home, a mitzvah you gave a level different from the app’s, or whose nags you moved by hand, shows a small bell beside its name with how many notifications it sends in all during its time.

On the Time window card of a mitzvah’s screen every notification is a dot on the timeline: a reminder a gold dot with a bell, a nag an orange dot, and the last time to start a red diamond, over the band of the critical time. Drag a reminder along the timeline to change its time, and the smart nag moves with it. A nag drags too, and the mitzvah’s nags then stay where you put them until Reset notification timeline, or until you choose a level for it. Two notifications cannot be less than 10 minutes apart: while you drag, those stretches are marked, the dot does not enter them, and a line says why. When a move drops nags, or a notification falls in the quiet hours or on Shabbat, a line under the timeline says so too. A tap on a dot opens its details: what it is, when it goes out, how the notification will look, -5 and +5 minutes, a reminder’s full edit, and delete. A tap on an empty stretch of the timeline adds a reminder there. Zoom to the critical time brings the timeline close to the time where the nags sit together, and Notifications to be sent lists them all in order.

A mitzvah that is on always keeps at least one reminder: the last one cannot be deleted, and switching on a mitzvah with none asks you to set one first.

In the notification, Done marks the mitzvah without opening the app, and Open text opens its text when it has one. Tapping the notification opens the text, or the mitzvah's screen when there is none. Settings → Device and notifications → Permissions shows every permission the app needs and allows what is missing; on Android, Unrestricted battery takes one tap, so reminders arrive on time.

In Settings → Device and notifications you can choose No notifications before, No notifications after, or both. A notification due before the chosen hour arrives at that hour, and one due after it moves to the next day at the hour set under Send from, as long as the mitzvah's time is still open; otherwise it is not sent. The notice before Shabbat and Yom Tov and the check-in reminders after them always go out on time.

Reminders are set ahead for the coming days even if the app is not opened: about a month on Android and about a week and a half on iPhone, where from the third day each mitzvah keeps one reminder. On the last planned day a notification at 8 pm, or the nearest allowed hour when notification hours are limited, says your reminders are about to run out, and every time the app opens it plans again.

My mitzvot and custom mitzvot

Settings → My mitzvot lists every mitzvah by group, each with a switch, and tapping a row opens the mitzvah's screen. Mitzvot added in an update are switched on for you, and the days before that never count as missed. A mitzvah not customary for women is off for a woman and marked Not customary for women, and she can still switch it on. The More group also holds hilulot and Rosh Chodesh.

Seasonal mitzvot appear only in their time: the Omer count, Chanukah candles (the reminder names the night), Selichot in your nusach's season, and Ushpizin on the nights of Sukkot with that night's guest. Kiddush Levana appears once a month, from the first night it is said until the period ends, until it is marked. Some mitzvot depend on the nusach.

Add custom mitzvah at the bottom of the screen creates your own mitzvah: a name, an icon of your choice, hours (an end before the start runs into the next day), and a schedule of set weekdays, a range from date to date or a run of days. You choose whether to skip it on Shabbat and Yom Tov, its nagging level and how many minutes it takes, and add rich content: text, a blessing, a link or a text from the siddur. Saving opens the mitzvah’s screen with one reminder at the start of its time, and its reminders are managed there, on the notification timeline. From everywhere, My mitzvot included, a custom mitzvah opens its own screen, and Edit mitzvah at the top of the screen opens the form. When its range or streak ends it switches off, and its history is kept.

Shabbat and Yom Tov

On Shabbat and Yom Tov the app rests: from candle lighting until the exit, 30 minutes after sunset in Israel and 40 abroad, no notifications go out, and a quiet screen with the exit time covers the app. This is by design, and the app comes back by itself.

An hour before candle lighting a notification gives the lighting and exit times and, as switched on, the Omer count, hilulot, Rosh Chodesh and the Ushpizin of the nights inside the Shabbat or Yom Tov.

After Shabbat or Yom Tov the app opens a check-in to mark what was done in that time, and a strip on Home reminds you to mark. Notifications remind you at the exit, two hours later and the next evening. A mark there counts as made in time, with no question. You can mark until dawn after the first weekday; anything unmarked by then counts as missed. Shabbat and Yom Tov count in history and the streak like any other day.

The siddur

In the Siddur tab each group opens and closes with a tap on its title: first the texts whose time is open now (open each time you enter the tab), then the meal blessings, the prayers (Shacharit, Mincha and Maariv), the mitzvot (tefillin, the morning Shema, the morning blessings, candle lighting, havdalah and the Omer), then the other blessings and prayers by group. A text not said today is greyed out, with when it is said, and it still opens at any time, in the text of the next day it is said and without Done. Each text is set for the day: Hallel, Musaf and inserts such as Ya'aleh VeYavo appear on their days.

At the top of the reader: Jump to section, Minyan or Alone in prayers with minyan passages (Alone folds Kaddish, Barchu, Kedusha and the Torah reading), larger and smaller text, and auto-scroll at the speed you choose. A folded passage opens on tap. While you read, the controls fold into one fixed line with Back, the text's name, the auto-scroll button and a Tools button that opens every control; scrolling either way never opens them; a short tap on the text starts or stops auto-scroll; a quick double tap on the text opens or closes the controls, scrolling closes them, and they come back by themselves at the top of the text. When the app is in English, the source's translation appears under the Hebrew where it has one.

The star button saves the current place as a bookmark; when the text already has bookmarks it asks whether to add a new one or move one of them here, and Bookmarks shows Where you left off and the text's bookmarks, to jump to or delete. Next time the reader offers to continue where you left off: for a mitzvah's text (the prayers, candle lighting, havdalah, the Omer) on the same day only, for every other text always. Done at the end of the text marks its mitzvah, and in a mitzvah's text the settings button at the top opens the mitzvah's screen.

Settings → Prayer and siddur sets the nusach (Ashkenaz, Sefard, Edot HaMizrach, Chabad or Teiman Baladi), the Shema opinion (Gra or Magen Avraham, which decides when the morning Shema window ends; the mitzvah's screen and its Home card show both for reference), Minyan or Alone, the Siddur wording for a man or a woman, and scrolling. The siddur wording follows the gender in your profile: a woman reads Modeh Ani, the morning blessings and the other women's forms.

Settings → Prayer and siddur, Name verses and personal prayer in the Amidah: up to three verses, one for each name, said at the end of Elokai Netzor before the second Yihyu Leratzon. A verse is found by the name, or by its first and last letter, in Tehillim and in the Tanakh parts of Chok LeYisrael when it is installed, or typed; searching by name, a verse that also holds the name is marked recommended and listed first. A personal prayer in free text can be added too. The siddur shows both in place in every Amidah of Shacharit, Mincha and Maariv, set off in the accent colour as your own addition rather than the prayer text and always open, with a minyan or alone, with a button under the Amidah that leads to the setting.

Selichot download as an add-on in your nusach: until it is installed, its row in the siddur, its mitzvah screen and the More tab offer the download, and a week before the season Home offers it too. Changing your nusach offers the new nusach's Selichot.

Tehillim and Tikkun HaKlali

The whole book of Tehillim is one text, the same in every nusach, opened from the More tab or the General group of the siddur. Jump to section offers the prayer before, the seven days of the weekly division and the prayer after.

Tikkun HaKlali leads the General group of the siddur. In both, the reader remembers where you left off and offers to continue there every time, and bookmarks can be kept.

Chok LeYisrael and Shnayim Mikra

Chok LeYisrael is an add-on downloaded once, about 5 MB, from its mitzvah screen or the More tab, and after that it is available without an internet connection. When the download ends, the app asks whether to turn on reminders and tracking. When there is a new version of the text, the mitzvah screen offers the update, and the add-on can be deleted there too.

The reader shows today's portion of the week's parasha, with a row of days to pick another day and, in a double week, a choice of which parasha to learn. While the mitzvah is on, Done marks the day, and earlier unmarked days of the week appear as folded make-ups. Rashi and Bartenura show or fold by Commentary or Text only at the top of the reader or in Settings, and each one opens on tap. The parasha row picks any other parasha to read, without Done, and Back to this week's parasha returns; a bookmark or the last place in another parasha takes you back there.

The mitzvah is open for the learning day, from dawn to the next dawn, and until candle lighting before Shabbat and Yom Tov, with its default reminder at 09:00. Thursday night counts for Thursday or Friday, as you choose on the first Done or in Settings → Prayer and siddur. The text is from the Torat Emet database under CC BY-NC-SA 2.5; the full credit is in Settings → About and contact.

Shnayim Mikra Ve'Echad Targum is another add-on of the same kind, about 1.2 MB: the whole Torah with Targum Onkelos and Rashi, an aliyah a day, Sunday to Thursday one aliyah each and Friday the sixth and the seventh. Each verse appears twice and then its translation, with Rashi after it, shown or folded by Commentary or Text only as in Chok LeYisrael. In a double week both parashiyot are read. Done, the make-ups of earlier days and reading another parasha work as in Chok LeYisrael, and the default reminder is at 20:00. Both are in the siddur under Learning and in the More tab.

Daily halacha, the halachot of the festival, lashon hara and Shemirat HaLashon

Two learning mitzvot from one halacha book: the Daily Halacha, a portion every day, and the Halachot of the Festival, in the thirty days before Pesach, Shavuot and Sukkot, and in the days before Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Chanukah, Purim, Tisha B'Av and the fasts. The book follows the nusach: Kitzur Shulchan Aruch for Ashkenaz and Sefard, Ben Ish Chai for Edot HaMizrach, and the Rambam's Mishneh Torah, a chapter a day, for Chabad and Teiman. Settings → Prayer and siddur offers another book, and for Ben Ish Chai a swap between its two years.

The book is an add-on downloaded once from the mitzvah screen, the More tab or the siddur under Learning, and after that it is available without an internet connection; when the download ends, the app asks whether to turn both mitzvot on. In English, the translation of Kitzur Shulchan Aruch can be downloaded too.

The reader shows the day's learning, and a row of days reads another day, without Done. While the mitzvah is on, Done marks the day, and each of the seven days before it left unmarked appears as a folded make-up. The mitzvot are open from dawn to the next dawn, on Shabbat and Yom Tov too, which are asked about after them, with default reminders at 08:00 for the daily halacha and 19:00 for the festival's halachot.

Laws of Lashon Hara is a third learning mitzvah, for every nusach and whatever book was chosen: Sefer Chofetz Chaim by its daily luach, three cycles a year, through the preface, the introduction, the laws of lashon hara, the laws of rechilut and the illustrations. The book is an add-on of its own, downloaded from the same places, with an English translation, and the reader, Done and the make-ups work as for the daily halacha. Its default reminder is at 13:00.

Shemirat HaLashon is a fourth learning mitzvah, for every nusach: the Chofetz Chaim's Sefer Shemirat HaLashon, the Gates of Remembering, Discernment and Torah and Book II, once a year by the daily luach. It is an add-on of its own too, with an English translation, and its default reminder is at 20:30.

Tracking, streak and the weekly report

The Tracking tab shows your streak and the days left to the next goal above three tabs. Summary shows how much you marked today, this week and this Hebrew month, a calendar of the Hebrew month where each day is shaded by how much of it was done (tap a day to see its mitzvot), and each mitzvah's consistency and streak. Tapping a mitzvah, in the list or inside a day, opens its screen. A mitzvah switched on today reads New until one of its days is counted.

At the end of Summary, the Reading in the siddur card counts how many times you read each siddur text (Grace After Meals, Nishmat, the traveler’s prayer and the rest) and how many Tehillim chapters, this week, this month and in all, with no streak. Each psalm counts on its own, Tikkun HaKlali’s chapters included, and once all 150 chapters are read a whole book is counted. Each text ends with an “I read it” button and each psalm with “I read this psalm” (Done counts as a read too). Without a press the smart count does it for you: by how long the text was in front of you for its length, not by scrolling fast to the end.

Trends shows the last 12 weeks, your strongest day of the week (Shabbat and Yom Tov apart, since they are marked in the check-in), how each mitzvah changed from the 30 days before, and the weekly reports. Achievements keeps the badges you earned and your records, even after old history is cleared.

The streak counts days in a row with at least one mitzvah marked. A day with nothing marked and something missed breaks it; a Shabbat or Yom Tov still waiting for its check-in does not.

A mitzvah whose time has ended can still be marked: in My day on Home, on the mitzvah's screen and in the siddur. The app asks when you did it. "I did it in time and didn't mark it" counts like a mark made in time. "I did it after the time passed" counts as done everywhere, in percentages, the month calendar, Trends, the weekly report and badges, and in My day on Home it shows in green like everything done, with an orange Done late label. Summary shows how many marks were on time in each period, the late marks of each mitzvah, and a To improve card with the mitzvah you were late on most in the last 30 days, which opens Late marks: which mitzvah, on which day, when its time ended and when you marked it. Trends shows the late marks of the last 12 weeks, and the weekly report the mitzvah you were late on most that week. The badges A week on time and A month on time need every mark in time, and Fewer late marks is given for a week with fewer late marks than the week before it.

A weekly report opens from the reports list in Trends: what was marked, the best day, which mitzvah to strengthen and what the next week holds, with buttons to move between weeks. About 45 minutes after Shabbat ends a notification says the report is ready; it can be turned off in Settings → Device and notifications.

The schedule

The schedule opens from the More tab, by day, week or month, with arrows to the previous and next. The day view lists the day's zmanim and mitzvot by the hour, and tapping a mitzvah opens it.

Tapping a day in the week or month view opens that day's mitzvot, with arrows to the previous and next day and a count of what was done. That screen is for reading: marks are made on Home, or in the check-in after Shabbat and Yom Tov.

Rosh Chodesh and hilulot

Rosh Chodesh notices and hilula notices are off by default. Switch them on in Settings → My mitzvot, in the More group, or on their own screens.

Each screen lists its notices: for Rosh Chodesh an early notice, the evening before and on the day; for a hilula an early notice and as it begins. Each can be turned off or timed: at or before sunset, or at a set time, and the early notice also how many days ahead. While the notices are on, at least one stays on. The coming dates are listed below. Tapping a tzaddik in the list opens a page about him, and so does tapping the hilula notice.

A notice that falls on Shabbat or Yom Tov is folded into the notification before it begins. These are notices, not mitzvot, so they do not count in history.

Family purity

Family purity tracking is offered only to married users who answered the gender question, and is switched on in Settings → Profile → Family purity. It follows the count, the bedikot, the tevila night and the onot of separation, with reminders by the halachic times.

The family purity screen records an onset, the hefsek tahara, bedikot and the tevila, and shows today's tasks, the coming onot and the month's calendar. Taharah settings choose the custom, who uses it (a woman, or a husband who sees only the onot and the tevila night), discreet notification wording and a biometric lock.

The data is stored encrypted and is not part of the backup. Delete the records starts a new count, and Reset family purity tracking also deletes the settings and turns tracking off. The app does not rule on halacha: on any doubt it stops the count until the rav's instruction is recorded.

Hours without notifications never delay the reminders of the hefsek, the evening bedika, the onah bedika, the tevila and separation; the morning bedika moves to the chosen hour only when that is before midday.

Settings: search, backup and reset

Settings opens with a search: type a word such as “notifications”, “nusach” or “backup” to go straight to the setting. It finds anything in the app: screens, mitzvot and categories, prayers and the passages and phrases inside them, tzaddikim by name, works or grave, the rabbis of the halacha hotline, guide topics, and your badges and figures with their values. It forgives typing on the other language's keyboard. Close results show at once, the search inside the prayers joins a moment later, and results found only despite a misspelling show apart, under “Did you mean”. The same search sits at the top of the More tab, and a setting found there opens in Settings at its place.

Its sections: Profile (name, phone, gender and marital status), Prayer and siddur, Halachic times (which times Home's carousel and circle show, and your favourites), Device and notifications (my location or a place you chose, either way back and forth, notifications, the weekly report, hours without notifications, permissions, theme, display size and language), Backup, restore and reset, and About and contact. The place also decides whether you count as in Israel or abroad, for example for the second day of Yom Tov. To remove the location permission, choose A place I chose and turn the permission off in the phone's settings; the search at the top of Settings finds localities and cities too and opens the choice on them.

On Android your settings, mitzvot and history are backed up to your Google account, and on iPhone in the device's iCloud backup. Settings → Backup, restore and reset also exports a backup file and restores it on any phone; a restore replaces what is on the phone. Reset the app deletes everything, including the family purity data, which no backup holds.

Updates

Most updates arrive by themselves: the app checks for one each time it opens or comes back, and downloads it in the background. When it is ready a strip says “A new version of the app is ready”, and tapping it loads it. Even with the app in the background, a notification says when an update is ready, while notifications are on, never on Shabbat or Yom Tov and never in hours without notifications.

The version number is in Settings → About and contact, as version-update number. What changed in each update is under What's new at the top of this guide. Changes that need a new version of the app come through the store.

About and contact

Settings → About and contact holds the version, an important notice on the accuracy of the times, a contact form (name, phone and message, sent to us through Formspree with the app version and device type), the email 365jew@gmail.com, and links to the support page, the privacy policy, the app's store page and the Chok LeYisrael credit.

For a halachic question, the halacha hotline in the More tab dials a line by the nusach in Settings: Ashkenazi lines, and for Edot HaMizrach and Teiman a line following Maran, by WhatsApp too.

The app’s notifications have sounds of their own, so you know at once they are from Jew 365: twelve to choose from, eight of them soft and gentle, and a separate sound for each kind of notification: the plain one (Bells by default), the orange one before the last (Kalimba) and the last one with the exclamation mark (Music box). Choose them in Settings → Device and notifications → Notification sound, and a choice plays it. On Android the sound can also be changed in the phone’s notification settings.

The app does not rule on halacha. The times and rules it applies are given to a rav for review, and each answer changes that one rule. In any doubt, ask a rav.
